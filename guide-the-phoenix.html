The Phoenix: How to Spot a Solar Company That Has Repeatedly Bankrupted

Identifying companies that have bankrupted and restarted under the same name — and why it matters for your warranty.

Solar Sensei · September 2026

The Orphan Problem

How many Australian solar companies have bankrupted to date? The figures translate to projections that more than one third of the 2.1 million systems in Australia are "orphans".

What is an orphan system?

A system unsupported by the original installation company — and often the manufacturer too. Owners of orphan systems must rely on other solar companies to fix faults on unfamiliar equipment. Many are installed so poorly that the only remedy is to pull the system off and start again.

What is a Phoenix Company?

A "phoenixed" company — rising from the ashes — is a rebirth. That rebirth is the result of a business bankrupting, being liquidated, and then transferring its business name to a fresh company in order to continue operations without too much of a blip to an external observer.

A Business Name is not a trading entity, and can be easily transferred between entities. A business can repeatedly bankrupt, voiding warranty obligations in the process — usually burning their suppliers, leaving unpaid debts, and similarly their subcontractors.

Who gets left behind?

Consumers who have paid a deposit and are awaiting installation, or those previously installed by the business. It's a clean slate for the phoenix — any obligations do not carry forward. And businesses that have done it once usually repeat, sometimes numerous times — each time escaping obligations to fix system issues, repair damaged property, and honour warranties.

The Subcontractor Problem

When a solar business exclusively outsources their install work, subcontractors are competing for work based on price. The lowest offer wins out much of the time. By attempting to minimise price, the business will be inherently motivated to engage the contractors willing to work for low cost — but unfortunately these are likely to be inexperienced installers almost assured to do sub-par work.

Quality Has a Cost

When a solar business is motivated to install a properly functioning and compliant system — and maintain a high standard of install and ongoing customer service — there is a cost involved, naturally.

Seeing a quote from a competitor which is literally only hundreds of dollars over the cost of equipment, transport, install, and incidentals — it is apparent that business will be cutting every possible corner, both relating to the install and future services availed.

The Double Whammy

Preferencing low-cost systems creates a double problem — homeowners end up with a system that is much more likely to have issues at some point, and a business that is far less likely to be able to assist when they do.

Apples to Apples?

Two quotes that look similar on paper can be worlds apart in practice. The real comparison isn't just the price — it's who's installing, how they install, and whether they'll be there when you need them.

How to Check for a Phoenix

The goal is to ascertain whether the business name has been transferred. Ideally you want to see the business name held by the same trading entity over the entire life of the business to date.

1

Search the business name via ABN Lookup

The results will identify the trading entity currently holding the business name. Note: a company can trade in its own name without separately registering it as a business name. A restructure can sometimes justify a change of trading entity — but in practice, the majority of examples indicate the business has bankrupted and the name transferred to a new entity to continue trading.

Additional step

In some cases, an additional step is needed — checking customer review history to determine how long the business has actually been operating under that name, especially when they claim "X years in business" on their website. The broader check is of great value and the process is straightforward.

Before You Sign: Quick Checklist

  • Search the business name on ABN Lookup and note the trading entity
  • Check how long that entity has held the business name
  • Search customer reviews to verify how long they've actually operated under that name
  • Look for multiple entities associated with the same business name over time
  • Ask directly: "Has this business name ever been held by a different entity?"
  • Verify the electrical contractor licence matches the trading entity — not just the name

Frequently asked questions

What is a phoenix solar company?

A phoenix company is a business that has bankrupted, been liquidated, and then transferred its business name to a new entity to continue trading. The new entity has no obligation to honour warranties or fix issues from the previous installation work.

How common are orphan solar systems in Australia?

Projections indicate that more than one third of the 2.1 million solar systems in Australia are "orphans" — systems unsupported by the original installation company. This is largely driven by the high rate of solar company bankruptcies.

How do I check if a solar company is a phoenix?

Search the business name on ABN Lookup (abr.business.gov.au) to identify the trading entity. Check how long that entity has held the name, and cross-reference with customer reviews to verify how long the business has actually been operating under that name.

Can a phoenix company still honour my warranty?

No. When a business bankrupts, the new entity has no legal obligation to honour warranties from the previous entity. This is one of the key risks — homeowners are left with a system and no recourse if something goes wrong.

Why do phoenix companies keep reappearing?

Business names are not trading entities and can be easily transferred. A business can repeatedly bankrupt, voiding warranty obligations each time, while continuing to trade under the same name. The practice burns suppliers, subcontractors, and consumers — but the name lives on.

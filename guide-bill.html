How to read your electricity bill for solar & battery analysis

Most homeowners check their bill to see what they owe. If you're considering solar panels or battery storage, it contains far more valuable information — and knowing where to look changes the quality of every decision that follows.

Your bill holds the key to better solar decisions

Your electricity bill is a record of how your home actually uses energy. Understanding these details is the first step toward an informed solar or battery decision, because it reveals:

  • How much energy your household consumes
  • When you use electricity (if you have time-of-use pricing)
  • What you pay for imports from the grid
  • What you receive for exported solar energy
  • Whether your current tariff structure supports solar or battery storage

1 Total consumption (kWh)

Your bill shows how many kilowatt-hours (kWh) your household consumed during the billing period — typically labelled Total usage or Consumption, sometimes broken down by peak, shoulder and off-peak periods if you have time-of-use pricing.

WHY IT MATTERS

Solar and battery systems are sized in part based on your consumption. A household using 20 kWh per day has different requirements to one using 40 kWh per day.

2 Tariff type

Check whether your bill shows a flat rate (a single rate for all usage), time-of-use (different rates for peak, shoulder and off-peak), or a controlled load (separate metering for appliances like hot water).

WHY IT MATTERS

Time-of-use tariffs can significantly affect the value of solar and battery storage. Batteries may be more valuable if you can charge during off-peak periods and discharge during peak periods — our time-of-use tariff guide explains this in detail.

3 Usage rates (cents per kWh)

This includes your peak rate (highest, typically evening), shoulder rate (mid-range, daytime or early evening), off-peak rate (lowest, overnight), and the supply charge — a daily fixed cost independent of usage.

WHY IT MATTERS

The difference between your import rate and your feed-in tariff determines how valuable self-consumption is. A larger gap makes solar and battery storage more attractive.

4 Feed-in tariff (if you have solar)

With existing solar, your bill shows how much energy you exported to the grid and the rate you were paid for it.

WHY IT MATTERS

Low feed-in tariffs (around 5–10¢ per kWh) compared to high import rates (around 25–35¢ per kWh) increase the value of using your own solar generation rather than exporting it.

5 Billing period & seasonal variation

Check the dates on your bill. Consumption varies significantly between summer and winter due to heating, cooling and daylight hours.

WHY IT MATTERS

A single bill may not represent your annual usage. Solar and battery analysis should consider seasonal variation to avoid over- or under-sizing systems.

6 Controlled loads & special tariffs

Some bills show separate charges for controlled loads such as hot water, electric vehicle tariffs with special overnight rates, or demand charges for larger households.

WHY IT MATTERS

These can affect whether battery storage or load-shifting strategies make financial sense for your home.

What your bill does not tell you

Three blind spots worth knowing

  • No interval data — most bills show total consumption for the period, not when you used electricity throughout the day
  • No solar generation data — with existing solar, bills show exports but not total generation or self-consumption
  • No future projection — bills reflect past consumption, not planned changes such as an EV, pool or heat pump

Frequently asked questions

Where do I find my usage rate on my electricity bill?

Look for a section labelled "Usage rates", "Tariff rates" or "Charge details". It will show your peak, shoulder and off-peak rates (if applicable) in cents per kWh, plus any daily supply charge.

How many bills do I need for a solar analysis?

Three to four recent bills help capture seasonal variation. If you can download 12 months of data from your retailer's online portal, that provides the most accurate picture.

What is a controlled load tariff?

A controlled load is a separate metered supply for specific appliances — typically hot water systems. It appears as a distinct line item on your bill with its own rate and consumption figure.

Does my bill show when I use electricity?

Most bills only show total consumption for the period. Time-of-use bills may break usage into peak, shoulder and off-peak bands, but not the specific times. Interval data (available from smart meters) provides the full picture.
